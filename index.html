About

I am a PhD candidate at the Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) and work in the Accelerators and Communications for HPC (AccelCom) group of the Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC). My research is about integrating quantum processors into high-performance computing: how to schedule, distribute and orchestrate quantum workloads alongside classical resources in a supercomputing centre.

My background combines physics and computer engineering: an MSc in Computer Engineering, studies in Advanced Physics and Data Science, and the MIT MicroMasters in Statistics and Data Science. Before the PhD I worked as a data scientist and machine learning engineer on cloud-native (AWS) systems.

Research lines

What I am working on now. Published work links to its paper; ongoing work is described by the question it addresses. The research map shows how these lines connect.

Quantum work under HPC resource management

Public Preprint and ACM ICS 2026 poster

Quantum jobs usually bypass the scheduler that runs the rest of a supercomputer. I build runtimes that turn quantum work into units the centre’s resource manager can queue, route and recover, across local QPUs, remote QPUs and classical simulators.

See the publication · Try the interactive explainer

Adaptive resources for quantum workloads

Ongoing Manuscripts in preparation

Large quantum simulations and long hybrid quantum–classical campaigns need different resources at different moments, and a shared machine changes what it can offer over time. This line studies how such workloads can adapt to the allocation available while they run, and survive interruptions.

Data movement in distributed quantum simulation

Ongoing

When a quantum state is spread across many nodes, communication between them often dominates the runtime. I study which decisions taken before a run determine that cost, and how a system can make them automatically.

The integration layer of quantum-centric supercomputing

Doctoral thesis In progress

Coupling quantum processors to a supercomputer raises a systems question: where should coordination between classical and quantum resources live, and what should it cost? My doctoral thesis addresses this layer.

  1. 2026

    Communication-Aware Qubit Placement and Automatic Node-Count Allocation for Distributed State-Vector Simulation

    Íñigo Aréjula-Aísa, Sergio Iserte, Petter Sandås, Ricard S. Raigada-García, Antonio J. Peña

    arXiv:2610.09659 [cs.DC]

    A communication-aware qubit-layout framework that reduces inter-node data exchange while selecting the number of computational nodes automatically. Integrated into QuCSLO, it supports dynamic node scaling, OpenQASM parsing and QuEST; evaluations on synthetic and QASMBench circuits show fewer communication cuts and runtime speedups above 2× over identity layouts, particularly for highly entangled circuits.

    Wave-Based Dispatch for Circuit Cutting in Hybrid HPC–Quantum Systems

    Ricard S. García-Raigada, Josep Jorba, Sergio Iserte

    arXiv:2604.15279 [cs.DC]

    DQR (Dynamic Queue Router), a runtime that treats circuit-cutting fragments as schedulable units, so HPC centres can apply standard resource management to NISQ workloads. Evaluated on the CESGA Qmio supercomputer with its local QPU and the remote IBM Torino backend, with per-fragment failover to classical simulators.
