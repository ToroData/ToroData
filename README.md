## About

I am a PhD candidate at the **Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC)** and the **Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC)**, where I am part of the *Accelerators and Communications for HPC* (AccelCom) group. My research focuses on the integration of **quantum processors into high-performance computing**: how to schedule, distribute and orchestrate quantum workloads alongside classical resources in a supercomputing centre.

My background combines physics and computer engineering: an MSc in Computer Engineering, studies in Advanced Physics and Data Science, and the MIT MicroMasters in Statistics and Data Science. Before the PhD I worked as a data scientist and machine learning engineer on cloud-native (AWS) systems.

### Research interests

`Hybrid HPC–quantum computing` `Quantum circuit cutting` `Scheduling & resource management` `Distributed quantum workloads` `Quantum software stacks` `Machine learning for systems`

## Research lines

- **Quantum work under HPC resource management** (public: preprint and ICS 2026 poster). Runtimes that turn quantum work into units a centre's resource manager can queue, route and recover.
- **Adaptive resources for quantum workloads** (ongoing). How large quantum simulations and hybrid campaigns adapt to the allocation available while they run.
- **Data movement in distributed quantum simulation** (ongoing). Which decisions taken before a run determine communication cost.
- **The integration layer of quantum-centric supercomputing** (doctoral thesis, in progress).

Interactive [research map](https://raigada-garcia.dev/research-map.html).

### Preprints

- **2026** — **Communication-Aware Qubit Placement and Automatic Node-Count Allocation for Distributed State-Vector Simulation**\
  Íñigo Aréjula-Aísa, Sergio Iserte, Petter Sandås, ***R. S. Raigada-García***, Antonio J. Peña\
  *arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.09659 · 7 October 2026*\
  A communication-aware qubit-layout framework that reduces inter-node data exchange while selecting the number of computational nodes automatically. Integrated into QuCSLO, it supports dynamic node scaling, OpenQASM parsing and QuEST; evaluations on synthetic and QASMBench circuits show fewer communication cuts and runtime speedups above 2× over identity layouts, particularly for highly entangled circuits.\
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09659) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09659)

- **2026** — **Wave-Based Dispatch for Circuit Cutting in Hybrid HPC–Quantum Systems**  
  ***R. S. Raigada-García***, J. Jorba, S. Iserte  
  *arXiv:2604.15279 [cs.DC]*  
  DQR (Dynamic Queue Router), a runtime that treats circuit-cutting fragments as schedulable units, so HPC centres can apply standard resource management to NISQ workloads. Evaluated on the CESGA Qmio supercomputer with its local QPU and the remote IBM Torino backend, with per-fragment failover to classical simulators.  
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15279) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15279)
